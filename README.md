# E-Commerce Orders- Power-BI Project
Wrapped up a Power BI project analyzing sales, targets, and customer behavior across a 15-country dataset. Sharing the full set of insights that came out of it:

Target Performance Total actual sales landed at $12.28M against a $12.81M target — a ~4% shortfall overall. But that average hides real variation: roughly half the sales POCs missed their target while the other half exceeded it, and those who fell short missed by an average of $21.7K each. At the team level, Alpha and Beta carried the largest total target allocations, while individual managers like Noah Meier and Benjamin Chen exceeded their targets by 7–8%.

Concentration at the Top
One sales manager led nearly every country outright — highest revenue, most orders, and most customers in the US, France, India, Spain, and Australia alike. At the individual contributor level, one sales POC alone generated over $1M in actual sales, more than 300K ahead of the next-highest performer — a striking gap worth understanding further.

Customer Category Patterns
Customer categories were evenly split in population (~500 customers each) and remarkably close on both average age and total sales — no single category stood out on either front. But one metric broke that pattern sharply: while 24–31% of customers in most categories have never placed an order, one category showed a 100% non-conversion rate, a red flag distinct from every other segment. Interestingly, that same evenness carried into sales-team coverage too — every customer category was served by an identical number of reps within the largest sales team.

Geographic & Channel Behavior
Order volume and average order value both varied meaningfully by country, with a small number of markets driving disproportionate order counts. On channel usage, Website narrowly led overall, but that aggregate masked a country-level reality — a different channel was actually the top choice in several individual markets, showing that a one-size-fits-all channel strategy would miss real regional differences.

Demand Seasonality
Monthly order volume and value both showed clear peaks and troughs across the year rather than a flat trend, and order value in particular swung significantly month to month — a useful signal for timing promotions or planning capacity around the strongest periods.
